# Tally
 
Split expenses without the arguments. Tally helps a group share costs fairly: a trip, a dinner, a flat, anything where one
person pays and everyone chips in. You add who's involved and what was spent, and Tally
works out exactly who owes whom, down to the last paisa.
 
Everything happens in your browser. There's no sign-up, no account, and nothing you
type is sent anywhere or stored anywhere. Your data exists only on your screen until
*you* choose to save it.
 
## Getting started
 
1. **Add the people.** Type each name and press Add. Names are all Tally needs.
2. **Add the expenses.** For each one, enter a description, the amount, and who paid.
3. **Read the results.** Balances and a "who pays whom" list update instantly as you type.
That's the whole loop. The sections below cover what each part can do.
 
## Adding expenses
 
Every expense has a description, an amount, the person who paid, and the people it was
for.
 
**Who was it for?** By default, everyone. Untick anyone who wasn't part of that
expense. If two people skipped the museum, only the visitors share the ticket cost.
The "Everyone" and "Clear" buttons save clicking when the group is large.
 
**How should it be divided?** Four ways:
 
| Split type | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Equally** | The usual case: everyone included pays the same. |
| **By exact amount** | You know each person's portion, for example from an itemised bill. The portions must add up to the total, and Tally tells you how much is missing or over until they do. |
| **By percentage** | Each person covers a stated percentage. Must total 100%. |
| **By shares** | Weighted splitting. Give a couple 2 shares and a solo traveller 1, and the couple pays twice as much. |
 
As you type, the card shows exactly what each person owes on that expense, so you can
sanity-check every bill on the spot. The person who paid is marked with a small
**paid** tag. They're still charged their own portion, because paying for dinner
doesn't mean you didn't eat.
 
**Leaving an expense out.** Every expense has an **Include** switch. Untick it and the
expense stays on screen (greyed and crossed out) but stops counting. This is handy for
asking "what would the numbers look like without this?" without deleting anything. The
expense counter shows "5 of 6" so you always know something is switched off.
 
## Reading the results
 
**Participants.** Each person shows three numbers: **paid** (what they put in),
**share** (their portion of everything they were part of), and the bold figure on the
right, which is the difference. Green means the group owes them; red means they owe
the group. Click the paid/share pill to expand a small table showing exactly which
expenses those totals come from.
 
**Who's up, who's down.** The same balances as a bar chart, giving a quick visual of
how lopsided things currently are.
 
**Settle up.** The list of payments that clears everything. Two styles, switchable at
the top of the panel:
 
- **Pay who you owe** *(the default)*: you repay the person who actually covered your
  share. If two people owe each other, Tally cancels it down to a single payment.
  Click the arrow on any payment to see exactly which bills it's made of. Useful when
  someone asks "why do I owe you ₹191?"
- **Fewest payments**: ignores who paid for what and clears all the balances in as
  few transfers as possible. You might pay someone you never bought anything from, but
  the group settles faster. The arrow here explains the balances behind each payment
  instead.
Both styles settle the same debts. The only difference is who hands money to whom.
 
## Saving and sharing
 
The toolbar at the top is split into two labelled groups, because they do different jobs:
 
**Your split (come back to it later)**
 
- **Save this split** stores your participants and expenses in a file on your device.
- **Open a saved split** brings it back so you can carry on editing. (Opening replaces
  whatever is currently on screen.)
- After the first save, **Save changes** appears. It updates the same file without
  asking where to put it again.
This file is your only copy: Tally deliberately doesn't remember anything between
visits, so **save before closing the tab** if you want to keep a split.
 
**The result (to share or keep)**
 
- **Copy summary** puts the whole thing on your clipboard as plain text, ready to
  paste into any chat: expenses, balances, and payments.
- The small copy icon in the Settle up panel copies **just the payment list**, which
  is usually all the group chat needs.
- **Export to Excel** creates a spreadsheet with the full picture: an overview, each
  person's balance, every expense, every individual share, and the settle-up plan.
  It's for reading and sharing. It can't be opened back into Tally, so use *Save this
  split* for anything you want to edit later.
## Little things worth knowing
 
- **Undo and redo**: the ↶ and ↷ buttons, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z (Cmd on Mac).
  Every change can be taken back, including Start over.
- **Start over** clears everything for a fresh split. It asks you to click twice so a
  stray tap can't wipe your work, and even then, undo brings it back.
- **Currency**: pick ₹, $, € or £ at the top right. It applies everywhere, including
  the Excel export.
- **The math is exact.** Split ₹100 three ways and Tally gives 33.34 / 33.33 / 33.33.
  The shares always add back to the exact bill, and the group's balances always cancel
  out to zero. No stray paisa ever appears or disappears.
- **Incomplete expenses don't sneak in.** If exact amounts don't add up, or
  percentages don't reach 100, that expense is left out of the balances and clearly
  flagged until you fix it, never silently rounded.
- **A person who paid for things can't be removed** until you reassign their expenses.
  Otherwise money would vanish from the books.
## Frequently asked
 
**Where does my data go?**
Nowhere. It lives in the page while it's open. Saving writes a file to your own
device; nothing is uploaded.
 
**I closed the tab. Is my split gone?**
Yes, unless you saved it. That's a deliberate choice: no hidden copies. Use *Save this
split* and reopen the file next time.
 
**Why does the payer owe money on their own expense?**
Because they consumed part of it. If abhi pays ₹2,670 for a dinner all four ate, he's
out ₹2,670 but also ate ₹667.50 of it, so the group owes him ₹2,002.50, not the full
amount.
 
**Why am I paying someone I never bought anything from?**
You've selected *Fewest payments*. That style only looks at overall balances, not at
who paid for what. It's faster for the group but less intuitive. Switch to *Pay who
you owe* if you'd rather every payment trace back to actual bills.
 
**The save dialog didn't appear.**
On Firefox and Safari the file is saved as a normal download instead, so check your
Downloads folder. Chrome and Edge show a proper save dialog and enable *Save changes*.
 
**Can several people edit the same split?**
Not at the same time. But the saved file can be sent to anyone, and they can open it,
add their expenses, and send it back.
